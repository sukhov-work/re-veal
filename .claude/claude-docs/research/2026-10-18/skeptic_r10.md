# Skeptic r10: the round-10 clips read as the owner will see them

None of the three clips is ready for his page. mismatch_5 lost the bright ring and the sky's vertical bands, but the bridge is still a hard bar, a dark halo sits around the sun, and a dome inside a grainy box appears at 1.8–2.2 s. mismatch_3 fixed the ceiling and the silver wheels, but the pane still crossfades: foliage lies over the crowd from 0.6 s, and the crowd is still visible through the trees at 1.8 s. mismatch_2 is worse than round 9. A flat pale fill covers up to 41 % of the frame at 1.4 s, and the man's face breaks into fragments and double exposures at 1.5–1.7 s.

Method, kept from skeptic_r9: I decoded all 90 frames of each clip with cv2, made contact sheets of every 6th frame at half size, and looked at single frames at full size (mismatch_3 at 1440x1080). I measured the mean absolute difference between consecutive frames ("step", in 8-bit levels) and compared the first and last frames with the photos, scaled to cover the canvas (A = `_S`, B = `_F`, as in `director_render.py:344`). I decoded the round-9 clips on disk the same way and compared them frame by frame. Times are frame/30. Coordinates are in canvas pixels.

- The round-9 mismatch_2 clip on disk does not match skeptic_r9's description (that report has a jump at frame 44 and a black void at frames 44–62; on disk the jump is at frame 51, 14.7). I compared against the clip on disk, which is presumably the re-render the owner graded (UNVERIFIED).
- I did not crop the generated keyframe's named changes (the closed hand, the sunglasses) on mismatch_3, and I did not check the other keyframes for added hands frame by frame. No third person was visible at viewing size.
- I did not check the code path of `vacated_first`, the matte fallbacks or the `keyframe_chain` timing. The causes named above are inferences.
